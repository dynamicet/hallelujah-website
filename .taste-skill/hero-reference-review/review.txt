Hallelujah Hospital hero — Taste review against the supplied references
27 October 2026

Scope
Review of the current local homepage at http://localhost:3000, including the
header because it contains the appointment action. Application files unchanged.
No direction was drawn: this is a review of one existing section.

Verdict
The hero has a strong visual foundation: large two-line type, a disciplined
deep teal/mint palette, an immediate doctor search and a warm family scene.
Its main weakness is hospital specificity. The words and image communicate
kindness more clearly than what Hallelujah can help a patient with.

ROUND 1
Seen in one second: a large care promise beside a family portrait on desktop;
a family portrait before the promise on a phone.
Idea (one sentence): the emblem's open arms become the curved frame around a
welcoming family scene and a direct route to finding a doctor.
Live thing: the labelled GET doctor-search form, with a browse-directory link.
Layout words by section: hero=split / phone hero=image above copy.
Scores: design 8.0 · usability 7.5 · creativity 6.0 · content 6.5 → overall 7.3.
Caps applied: creativity <=6 because the headline and family scene remain
applicable to another hospital when the logo is covered. The emblem-inspired
curves add identity, but that connection is not self-evident in the first view.

Top three problems (most visible first), with screenshot evidence and fixes:
1. The large image and promise establish warmth but provide little hospital-
   specific evidence (desktop-fold.jpg, mobile-fold.jpg). Preserve the warm
   direction; use an approved photograph of Hallelujah's care team, consultation
   or premises, and replace task-only supporting copy with one verified reason
   to choose this hospital. Location or relevant services can supply useful
   specificity. The present concept image should keep its provenance caption
   until replaced. No statistics, accreditations or care claims are proposed
   without evidence.
2. Appointment access loses prominence on phones (mobile-fold.jpg versus
   desktop-fold.jpg). Desktop and tablet have a visible header action; at
   390px it is inside Menu and absent from the first screen. Doctor search and
   Browse doctor profiles then occupy both visible action paths. Keep search
   primary, and add a plain Request an appointment link beneath it, or retain
   a compact appointment action in the mobile header. Preserve an easy route
   to browse the directory without entering a name.
3. The tablet image crops the younger woman's face at the right edge
   (tablet-fold.jpg). Use a tablet-specific crop or alternate image with both
   faces inside the safe area; inspect at 600, 768, 900 and 1024px after changing
   it. The family relationship is the picture's subject and needs both faces.

Fourteen-pass critique notes
1. One/five seconds: strong focal point; hospital identity and doctor search
   are understandable. Scope of care and reason to choose remain vague.
2. Squint: the teal text field and family image create two clear large masses.
3. Cover logo: competitors could use the same slogan and scene.
4. Edges: copy, search and browse link share a left edge; the frame is orderly.
5. Space: no accidental empty half or large hole in the hero.
6. Type: decisive scale; headline remains two lines at all three widths.
7. Colour: teal ground, white type and a mint action; coherent proportions.
8. Images: image supports welcome, not evidence of the hospital; tablet crop
   loses part of one face. Existing provenance is correctly disclosed.
9. Sameness: one hero only; below-fold component warnings are out of scope.
10. Phone: its own image-first composition, readable text, visible search;
    appointment entry requires opening the menu or scrolling.
11. Fullness: no wireframe-like empty region in the hero.
12. Substance: useful doctor access; limited specific hospital information.
13. Collisions: no horizontal overflow or broken image detected at 1440,
    768 or 390px; no visible element covering the headline or search.
14. Competitor/live thing: doctor search is useful; the visual idea is not yet
    distinctive enough to identify Hallelujah without its name.

Reference lessons
Sidra Medicine — https://www.sidra.org/
Captured in Chromium: references/sidra-desktop.jpg.
Its campus imagery immediately establishes the hospital setting, and service
entry points identify audiences: children/young people, women, adults. Borrow
the clarity about setting and care scope. Its translucent icon cards and dense
navigation conflict with Taste house rules and are not recommended for copying.

Mayo Clinic — https://www.mayoclinic.org/
The web reader returned the current homepage's short care headline, innovation
link, appointment action and doctor/location navigation. Chromium returned
Access Denied, recorded in references/mayo-desktop.jpg. No claims about its
current rendered composition, photography or mobile behavior are made here.
Borrow concise messaging and explicit action labels.

Cleveland Clinic Abu Dhabi — https://www.clevelandclinicabudhabi.ae/en
Captured in Chromium: references/cleveland-desktop.jpg.
Its doctor-patient scene establishes a clinical context, while its first-screen
entry points describe concrete tasks and audiences. Borrow task clarity and
the visual connection to care. Its icon-card row and floating call treatment
are not recommendations under Taste's house rules.
