## The argument

A person finding care needs to recognize both the hospital and the next task. Let color mark
the chapters of that journey: a human opening, confident care discovery, warmer visit preparation,
a moment to form a question, and a clear practical form. The page should change emphasis as the
visitor's purpose changes. Blue supplies continuity; orange and magenta supply meaningful changes
of tone. Paper, white, and the pale companions create space to read.

Earlier advice confined orange to a small supporting moment and magenta to an isolated flourish.
That was unnecessarily cautious. Supporting describes hierarchy, not a maximum area or a ban on
colored text, rules, boxes, or complete sections. Likewise, 75/15/10 is not an acceptance test.
It can describe one light composition, but it should not prevent a substantial dark-blue chapter,
an orange preparation field, or a magenta editorial panel when each earns its place.

The refinement keeps the curated anchor hues. It gives them more compositional responsibility.

## The study's chapter sequence

| Chapter | Field and type | Why this emphasis belongs here |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Header and human opening | Paper/white identity field; natural photography with separately tested dark scrim and white reading content | Establish the hospital and an immediately understandable next step. Preserve the photograph's human color. |
| Care discovery | Substantial deep-blue field, white and readable blue-air display type, white directory rows, and structural lines | Give finding care sufficient presence. This chapter should feel like the page's structural center, not another faint card. |
| Visit preparation | Pale-orange field with a deep-orange italic heading and orange-framed note | Make practical help warm and visible. A supporting family can own an entire useful section without replacing blue as the brand spine. |
| Questions and reflection | Pale-magenta field, deep-magenta headline, and full magenta quote box with white text | Give the question a memorable pause. Magenta expresses editorial emphasis, without encoding gender, a condition, or a specialty. |
| Form task | Pale-blue grouping with a white form, blue-air offset edge, and meaningful control boundaries; blue main action | Return to a predictable practical task. The offset edge is decorative; selected, error, success, and focus treatments must remain clear. |
| Urgent routing and close | Explicitly named red emergency treatment; deep-blue footer with inverse text | Distinguish urgent meaning from editorial warmth and close the page with the same blue identity. |

This sequence demonstrates a range; it is not a universal template for every route. A short
appointment page can be mostly paper and white. A long editorial page can give more space to
an accent family. Judge the hierarchy in context rather than counting colors.

## Rules for confident detail

- **Give type real scale.** Main headings should create a visible jump from body copy; chapter
  titles should retain that hierarchy on phones. Keep existing type families, and adjust measures
  and spacing to the content. Avoid shrinking essential copy to make a composition fit.
- **Make rhythm visible.** Alternate dense practical groups with open reading areas. Keep related
  label, copy, and action close together; leave more space before the next chapter. Repeated rows
  use repeated alignment and spacing, not a new decorative treatment for every item.
- **Use lines to explain structure.** A rule can separate directory rows, mark a section opening,
  or connect an editorial detail. Orange or magenta rules can be substantial. If a line is the only
  way to perceive a boundary or state, it must meet meaningful non-text contrast requirements.
- **Use boxes to hold a decision or task.** A white form, tinted preparation note, or editorial
  panel can have a clear edge and sufficient padding. Avoid both automatic card grids and an
  artificial ban on cards: containment is useful when it explains what belongs together.
- **Let shades carry hierarchy.** Deep orange on pale orange and deep magenta on pale magenta
  can carry complete readable passages. Saturated orange/magenta can carry a bar, marker, or bounded
  field. They are not restricted to tiny ornaments. Blue air can supply decorative fills with deep
  blue text and readable display text on a tested deep-blue field; it is not an additional action color.
- **Keep actions recognizable.** Blue/white remains the main action contract in this study;
  strong blue is hover and deep blue is pressed. White/inverse actions work on dark fields.
  Secondary actions can be outlined or underlined. A warm field does not require a warm CTA.
  Changing a primary action to an accent requires a clear local hierarchy and tested states.
- **Keep color subordinate to meaning.** Orange support does not imply warning, magenta does
  not label a patient group, and blue information does not automatically imply interactivity.
  Use labels, underlines, grouping, and explicit action language to resolve these distinctions.

## Pairings that remain binding

Expressiveness changes placement and scale; it does not change contrast requirements.

- Use white on primary/strong/deep blue for their appropriate solid treatments. Ordinary light-field
  text links use strong blue, not primary blue on blue mist. Inverse links need inverse colors.
- Do not use normal white text on orange `#F26522`; it is only about 3.15:1. Reading ink on
  that orange is also below 4.5:1, at about 4.32:1. Neither pairing is available for ordinary copy.
  Put the copy in a pale inset or use deep orange `#A8430C` on pale orange `#FFF0E7`.
- White on magenta `#D81B72` and deep magenta `#A61558` on pale magenta `#FCEAF2` are available
  readable pairings. Validate every hover, pressed, and surrounding-surface combination separately.
- White controls retain edge `#718892`; do not transfer that boundary unchanged onto blue veil,
  where its approximately 2.994:1 contrast misses the 3:1 requirement. Quiet edge is decorative.
- Keep the separated deep-blue focus ring on light surfaces and inverse focus treatment on dark
  ones. Rings must remain visible when they cross colored sections or the edges of boxes.
- Keep natural image color. Scrim opacity, reading position, responsive crop, and fallback surface
  require rendered review; a brand token alone cannot establish text-on-image contrast.

The risks to inspect are competing action emphasis, too many equal-weight chapter fields,
editorial accents mistaken for state meaning, and mobile type or focus clipped by containers.
Review the full page at desktop and phone widths, then test actual states. A successful study
should feel more assured because its hierarchy is clearer and its chapters have purpose.
