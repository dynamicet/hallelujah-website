## Observable acceptance

1. Persisted active synthetic identities receive author, reviewer, publisher or matching domain
   roles. Requests reload current roles; supplied/stale role claims cannot grant permission.
   Authors edit/submit only their own draft and cannot self-approve even when assigned review roles.
   A separate identity manager may change/deactivate other identities; self-role changes are denied.
2. New records start as unsigned drafts. Workflow commands require the current revision. Actors,
   timestamps and append-only audit entries are generated by the write boundary. Stable identity,
   content class, workflow metadata and prior audits cannot be supplied or rewritten.
3. Reviewers with medical, clinical, operations, human-resources, content, privacy or brand roles
   approve only matching domains. General approval requires every applicable review. Contact and
   emergency classes additionally require operations review and a distinct general approver;
   post-publication verification is recorded by independent operations. These are synthetic CMS
   states and cannot grant authentic safety approval or public eligibility.
4. Any edit follows a return to draft and clears earlier approvals. Content cannot change during
   approval/scheduling/publication. PostgreSQL transaction locking and current-revision checks
   reject concurrent stale writes. Approval identities/roles are rechecked before publication.
5. Scheduling uses complete UTC instants; missing/non-UTC, elapsed review/expiry and early publication
   fail. An internal lifecycle service explicitly advances due controlled records using a publisher and expires/withdraws them with a
   reason. No production scheduler is enabled. Retired records retain identity/audit and cannot be deleted.
6. Internal preview uses authenticated collection access with private/no-store and noindex directives.
   Anonymous preview/read/write and another author's preview fail; no HTTP surface is introduced.
7. Pure negative/transition checks, disposable Payload/PostgreSQL persistence and concurrent-write
   tests pass. Full repository, history-secret and dependency-audit gates pass. Hosted exact-head
   CI is required before merge; local CMS persistence is recorded independently of hosted checks.
