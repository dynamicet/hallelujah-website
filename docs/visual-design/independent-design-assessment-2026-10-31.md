# Independent visual and UX assessment

**Review date:** 31 October 2026\
**Overall assessment:** 6/10\
**Status:** Open recommendations; no application changes made during assessment\
**Evidence:** [Browser captures and interaction records](../../artifacts/independent-design-review/)

The running site has a strong desktop first impression, warm art direction, and a coherent teal identity. That quality is less consistent across the complete patient journey. Repeated homepage sections, generic imagery, sparse directory results, and unavailable practical information weaken the experience.

This is the central checklist for addressing the assessment. Check an item only after its acceptance criteria have been reviewed in the browser. The recommendations are independent design judgments, not replacements for hospital approvals or existing operational controls. Content awaiting verification must remain visibly unavailable; do not invent facts or activate contact and submission features to satisfy a design recommendation.

## Review coverage

The assessment used the running local website in Chromium at desktop **1440 × 900**, tablet **768 × 1024**, and mobile **390 × 844**. It captured 106 screenshots across the homepage, doctor directory and a sample profile, service directory and a sample detail page, Patients & Visitors, appointments, emergency information, feedback, referrals, health topics and a sample topic, and news/resources and a sample article.

Interaction evidence includes menu opening and Escape, search, alphabetical topic selection, the video dialog, patient-guide opening and topic selection, and the appointment care-to-details transition. A reduced-motion homepage capture is included. Sample detail pages represent the templates; every individual record was not reviewed. This was not a complete appointment-submission test or screen-reader/WCAG certification.

Full-page browser captures can reposition sticky headers and sidebars. Use viewport captures to judge overlap and placement; do not treat full-page capture artifacts as site defects.

## Preserve these strengths

- Desktop hero image, headline hierarchy, and clear primary appointment action.
- Teal, white, and mint foundation with generally strong primary-text separation.
- Confident display typography and restrained use of shadows and gradients.
- Patient-oriented language, clinician language information, and preparation tools.
- Mobile hero text separated from photography and appointment form placed before supporting imagery.
- The distinction between requesting an appointment and receiving confirmation.

## Critical improvements

### C1 Make emergency information usable before public launch

- [ ] Resolve and verify in the browser.
- **Observed problem:** The emergency page offers two illustrative routes, fake contact values, comparisons, metadata, and example directions. It requires interpretation without giving a dependable next action.
- **Why it matters:** Urgent visitors need immediate orientation and trustworthy contact and arrival information.
- **Change:** With hospital approval, lead with one clear emergency action and concise alternatives, followed by verified entrance and arrival details. Keep example routes outside the public emergency journey. Until verified, retain explicit unavailable states in the demo.
- **Expected result:** Faster comprehension and a credible urgent-help experience.
- **Acceptance:** The first mobile screen communicates the approved action and its purpose. Public contact and arrival details contain no examples; secondary explanation does not precede the main action. Hospital approval is recorded before release.
- **Evidence:** [Mobile emergency viewport](../../artifacts/independent-design-review/emergency-mobile-top.jpg), [desktop page](../../artifacts/independent-design-review/emergency/desktop-full.jpg).
- **Dependency:** Hospital verification and publication approval.

### C2 Replace illustrative trust evidence and operational information

- [ ] Resolve and verify in the browser.
- **Observed problem:** Fictional testimonials, illustrative clinician identities, prominent statistics, and unavailable contact and arrival facts do not substantiate the confidence of the presentation.
- **Why it matters:** Disclaimers explain the demo but do not establish public trust or help visitors plan care.
- **Change:** Replace public proof with approved clinician identities, authentic authorized imagery, verified statistics, and practical information. Remove fictional testimonials and unsupported proof sections from the public version. Keep current preview disclosures until approved replacements exist.
- **Expected result:** Credibility grounded in the actual hospital.
- **Acceptance:** Every public testimonial, clinician identity, statistic, operational claim, and contact detail has an accountable approval source. Unapproved material is omitted or clearly unavailable. Do not simply remove disclaimers while leaving example content.
- **Evidence:** [Homepage statistics and testimonials](../../artifacts/independent-design-review/home/desktop-page-01.jpg), [contact section](../../artifacts/independent-design-review/mobile-home-contact.jpg).
- **Dependency:** Hospital content and asset approval.

### C3 Stop Help from covering content and controls

- **Status, 1 November 2026:** Implementation addressed in `563b861` (`fix(web): restore compact floating Help launcher`); recorded browser regression passed at all three review widths.
- [ ] Complete the remaining physical-device on-screen keyboard check for final acceptance.
- **Observed problem:** The fixed Help control overlaps reading content, article actions, form content, and the hero play-control area at observed scroll positions.
- **Why it matters:** It interrupts reading and competes with the intended tap target.
- **Change:** Reposition or collapse it during reading and form tasks, reserve adequate clearance, and provide a clearly dismissible help panel.
- **Expected result:** Unobstructed reading and interaction.
- **Acceptance:** At all three review widths, scroll through each main journey and focus its controls; Help must not cover headings, links, fields, validation messages, or primary actions. Check the on-screen keyboard on a mobile device during implementation validation.

## High impact improvements

### H1 Shorten the homepage and remove repeated promises

- [x] Resolve and verify in the browser (local ready-state homepage, 1 November 2026).
- **Observed problem:** At 390px, the homepage measured approximately 15,334px tall, over 18 viewport heights. Searches, task choices, care-team links, visit sections, and emotional messages repeat.
- **Why it matters:** Length delays practical information and reduces the impact of the strongest sections.
- **Change:** Use the sequence: hospital introduction → find care → authentic evidence → prepare and arrive → contact. Merge overlapping visit sections, remove the follow-up banner, and retain a human story only when authentic evidence is available.
- **Expected result:** Stronger momentum and a clearer information hierarchy.
- **Acceptance:** Each retained section answers a distinct visitor question. Repeated sections require a stated independent purpose. Contact and arrival information move substantially earlier. Compare the full desktop and mobile scroll before and after; do not achieve brevity by making text smaller.
- **Evidence:** [Desktop overview](../../artifacts/independent-design-review/home/desktop-page-01.jpg), [middle sections](../../artifacts/independent-design-review/home/desktop-page-02.jpg), [later sections](../../artifacts/independent-design-review/home/desktop-page-03.jpg), [measured mobile height](../../artifacts/independent-design-review/final-checks.json).

**Implementation, 1 November 2026:** Following section-by-section evaluation, retained sections now serve distinct questions rather than following a fixed five-section limit. Direct doctor/service search remains in the hero; the three-choice navigator distinguishes a known doctor, a known service, and help choosing care. The health library starts with a concern, service highlights introduce selected areas, and clinician previews support comparison. Preparation, companions, support needs, arrival, contact availability, and emergency routing are consolidated into one earlier visit-planning block. Partners follow that block without implying insurance coverage. Statistics and testimonials retain their separate preview-only evidence roles; C2 remains open. The personal-care story and generic visit tabs become one shorter consultation section with question prompts and an explicitly illustrative image. The follow-up banner and redundant story/visit CTAs are removed. News remains last as timely reading, distinct from condition reference.
