H6 reassessment — 2 November 2026

Scope: current local preview at ddedbf7; review only, no application changes.
Browser: headless Chromium, 320×740, 390×844, 768×1024, 844×390 and 1440×900.
Evidence: results.json, followup.json, reduced-motion.json and named screenshots.
The assessment's original images remain historical evidence.

Disposition: H6 remains open, with narrower recommendations. Working keyboard
dismissal is verified; the Help launcher is not automatically a defect.

1. Initial mobile space remains a real issue. At 320 and 390px, the header itself
   is 176px: 92px utilities plus 84px identity/menu. A separate 51px preview notice
   brings the start of main content to y=227px (31% of a 740px viewport, 27% of
   844px). The original “roughly 225px header” conflates notice and header.
   The header hides on downward scrolling and returns on upward scrolling in
   portrait. Reduced motion keeps it visible. Short landscape uses a nonsticky
   header, which scrolls away normally. These behaviors help reading after entry;
   they do not reduce the initial footprint. Preserve the preview disclosure.
   At 768 and 844px the header is 140px; desktop is 152px.

2. The contact row is visually active despite all three controls being unavailable.
   Browser measurements confirm 44px targets and aria-disabled=true. In the
   screenshot the controls have normal ink, full-color marks and no visible
   unavailable label. Accessibility metadata and hover titles do not explain the
   individual state to a phone visitor before activation. Recommend moving
   unavailable secondary channels into contact/menu content, or visibly stating
   availability if this row is deliberately retained as a design preview.
   Keep Emergency information discoverable; its destination remains preview
   information, not a verified emergency contact channel.

3. Menu dismissal works but its visual state is ambiguous. Enter opens it,
   aria-expanded changes to true, Tab reaches Find a doctor, Escape closes it and
   returns focus to Menu at all four compact widths. Clicking Menu again closes
   it. There is still no close symbol in the panel and the trigger retains its
   hamburger and “Menu” label while open. Recommend an explicit Close control
   or an obvious open-state change. At 844×390 only 166px of a 412px menu is
   visible, so lower links require scrolling; this is a usability refinement,
   not proof those links are inaccessible.

4. Help is now a small two-choice launcher: Request an appointment and Chat with
   our guide. Guide access takes two activations. The appointment route is distinct,
   so the extra step has a reason. The launcher has both an internal close button
   and a trigger that changes to an X. Enter opens it, Escape restores focus to
   Help. The guide opens as a modal with a clear close control, starts focus there,
   retains focus inside after 16 forward Tabs, and Escape returns focus to Help
   at all five sizes. Guide bounds fit the viewport; this is not a claim that all
   content is initially visible. At 844×390 the transcript is cramped between
   fixed header, toolbar and composer; topic choices require internal scrolling.
   Keep the current launcher unless guide-first access is a product requirement.
   Consider short-landscape compaction separately from click-count reduction.

5. The menu still offers Find a doctor (/doctors) and Search doctors
   (/doctors#directory-search-title). These have distinct URLs but share the
   directory search task. The directory now supports name, specialty, condition
   and service search. At 390px the search heading starts at y=472px on the
   directory; the shortcut scrolls 280px and places it at y=192px. It therefore
   has a measurable bypass function. Treat consolidation as a low-priority
   navigation decision, not a confirmed defect. Keep contextual doctor routes in homepage search,
   care journeys and clinician profiles; repetition across those contexts alone
   is not evidence of unnecessary navigation.

Verification: no horizontal page overflow at the five tested sizes; no JavaScript
exceptions observed. Full-page inspector: 0 FAIL, 5 WARN (card rows, type-size
count, absent inline footage, sideways carousel and tablet alignment). All ten
inspector screenshots reviewed. These broad checks do not certify accessibility
or hospital contact readiness. No screen reader, physical phone, soft keyboard,
other browser engine or full automated focus-cycle audit was run.
